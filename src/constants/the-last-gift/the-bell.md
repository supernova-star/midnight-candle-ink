**Misti reached into the wooden box for the final time.**

Her fingers sliding past the empty spaces until they touched the last remaining pouch. She drew out the fourth envelope and its matching pouch.

The pouch felt much heavier and thicker than the first three. Misti unclasped the cloth flap and tilted it over her lap. Three heavy items slid out onto her dress with a quiet metal clink.

The first was a dome-shaped brass bell, hollow like an upside-down cup with a smooth hole drilled through its top. The second was an old brass door key with a dark green velvet ribbon tied through its bow. The third was a tiny, slender brass locking screw.

Misti picked up the key and traced its cold, sharp teeth with her thumb. Now, the strange collection of items laid out on the cool floor tiles finally made sense. She looked at the curved brass arm, the dark wood block, the steel spring, the shiny bell, the tiny screw, and this key. They were no longer random pieces. They were clearly parts of a real, working machine waiting to be built. Her chest felt tight with quiet wonder as she unfolded the paper inside.

> _My dear Misti,_
>
> _We have spent enough time today looking back at the map of where we've been. You have read my heart in those old memories, and now it is finally time to look forward to where you are going._
>
> _Take all these little pieces you've collected—every single piece of metal and wood, along with this key—and slip them safely into your coat pocket. Put on your coat, and step out into the evening air. Walk down toward Southern Avenue, right past that old bakery where the night always smells like warm bread and melting sugar._
>
> _Look for Number 42. It is that quiet corner building with the dark green shutters, hiding under the big branches of the banyan tree. When you finally stand before that heavy wooden front door, take a deep, steady breath. Don't let your mind talk you out of it, and don't look back. Just turn the key in the lock and step inside._
>
> _I'll be waiting for you on the other side,_
>
> _Your co-conspirator,_  
> _Bristi_

Misti’s heart gave a sudden, hard thump against her ribs. She picked up the dark green velvet ribbon and tied it tightly around her left wrist. The soft fabric rested against her skin. For a moment, she paused, holding the objects and anchoring herself in the immediate physical sensations.

 Then, she slid them deep into her coat pocket. Grabbing her umbrella, she hurried downstairs and stepped out into the evening. The cool drizzle brushed against her face, and the wet road reflected the yellow glow of the streetlamps as she set off toward Southern Avenue.
