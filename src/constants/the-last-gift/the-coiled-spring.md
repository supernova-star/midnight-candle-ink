**The sky outside turned a rich indigo as twilight settled quietly over Kolkata.**

A cool breeze drifted through the open balcony window, carrying the fresh scent of rain-washed soil and the distant murmur of returning traffic. Misti reached back into the wooden box, her fingers brushing against the remaining envelopes and pouches before drawing out the third envelope and its matching pouch.

As she unsealed it and opened the small cloth flap, out slid a delicate, tightly coiled steel spring with a tiny brass bead soldered onto the very tip.

She arranged the three items side by side on the cool floor tiles before her: the curved brass hook, the rectangular mahogany block, and the slender coiled spring with its tiny brass bead. 

Together, they looked like random, mysterious fragments from a disassembled clock or a forgotten music box. Smiling softly at her sister's fond penchant for secret puzzles, she opened the third letter. The rounded blue handwriting here was slightly lighter, but the words carried an undeniable, radiant tenderness.

> _My sweet sister,_
> 
> _This is the letter where I thank you. You have spent your entire adult life acting as my shield and my protector. Ever since Baba passed away, whenever life got hard or uncertain, you put your own personal ambitions into a dark drawer without a second thought._
>
> _You traded your paints, your sketchbooks, and your cozy bookstore-café floor plans for corporate spreadsheets and endless boardrooms, pretending your own happiness didn't matter as long as I was safe and taken care of. You carried the full weight of the world on your shoulders so that I could run free as an illustrator._
>
> _You bought my first sketchbooks, paid my tuition fees, cooked late-night meals when I was stressed, and listened patiently to every single one of my messy doubts while quietly swallowing your own deep fatigue. I saw every single sacrifice, Misti, even when you tried so hard to hide them behind a brave smile._
>
> _But turning thirty is your turning point, my dear sister. You don't have to keep pausing your life or putting your dreams on hold to protect everyone else. You have fulfilled every single promise you ever made to our family, and you have carried me far enough. It is finally okay to put down that heavy shield, step forward into the light, and choose your own happiness without feeling even a single shred of guilt._
>
> _Forever grateful,_  
> _Bristi_

A single warm tear spilled over Misti’s lashes, dropping onto the heavy cotton paper and dampening the blue ink into a faint smudge. 

She pressed the letter tightly against her chest and closed her eyes. The heavy, long-held knot near her heart slowly uncoiled, leaving behind a profound sense of quiet relief that she hadn't felt in years.
