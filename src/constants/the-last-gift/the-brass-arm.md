**The afternoon rain had just stopped, leaving the Kolkata sky heavy and slate-grey.**

The air drifting through the open window was warm and thick, carrying the damp scent of soil from the potted plants outside. Down in the lane, the muted honk of a yellow taxi echoed against the brick walls before fading into the humid quiet. Inside, the old ceiling fan spun with a low, rhythmic squeak.

Misti sat on the cool tile floor, her back resting against the foot of her bed frame. On her lap sat a small teakwood box tied with a faded green ribbon. A friend had handed it to her an hour ago, saying only that Bristi had left strict instructions for it to be delivered on the morning of Misti’s thirtieth birthday.

Misti untied the ribbon and lifted the heavy lid. Inside lay four ivory paper envelopes, each resting beside a small, soft fabric pouch. She picked up the first envelope and reached for its accompanying pouch. As she unclasped the small cloth flap, a smooth, heavy object slid out into her palm alongside the letter.

It was a curved piece of solid, polished brass with a threaded screw hole at one end. It looked like an unattached arm or a strange drawer pull. 

Puzzled, Misti traced the cool metal with her thumb before setting it on her knee and unfolding the note. The sight of her younger sister's rounded, familiar blue handwriting made her throat tighten with a quiet warmth.

> _My dearest Misti,_
>
> _Happy thirtieth birthday! I am willing to bet you are sitting on the bedroom floor right now, letting your ginger tea go cold while your mind races with work emails and corporate deadlines. Please take a sip before it turns completely cold._
>  
> _Do you remember the summer we turned twelve and ten? The street outside flooded up to our knees after three days of heavy rain. Ma strictly ordered us to stay indoors, but we snuck out anyway to buy hot snacks from the vendor at the corner. We completely ruined our matching green dresses, sauce dripped all over our wrists, and we laughed until our ribs hurt._
>  
> _You worried about those ruined dresses for weeks afterward, Misti, terrified of getting scolded. But I only remembered how bright and weightless you looked when you forgot to be careful. Promise me you will try to find that weightless feeling again today._
>
> _Love always,  
Bristi_

A faint, trembling laugh escaped Misti’s lips. She looked down at the clay mug resting beside her knee. A thin ribbon of steam was still rising from the surface. She picked it up, took a long, warming sip, and slipped the heavy brass piece deep into her dress pocket.
