**For the first time, the figure was close enough to see clearly.**

Maya stared at the photograph. The figure was standing beside the bed. Its face was still hidden in the darkness. But this time, Maya noticed something else.

A small silver ring on its hand.

She froze. _"Priya…"_

Priya looked at her. _"What?"_

_"That ring."_

Priya looked at the photograph again. _"What about it?"_

Maya swallowed. _"My mother had one exactly like that."_

Priya's expression changed. _"Are you sure?"_

Maya nodded slowly. _"I'm sure."_ She took the photograph and looked at it again. The ring was old and simple. Just like the one her mother used to wear.

For a moment, Maya couldn't speak. Then she noticed something else. The figure was wearing a long white nightdress.

Maya's hands went cold. Her mother used to wear one just like it.

Priya looked at her. _"Maya?"_

_"I remember that night,"_ Maya said.

Priya frowned. _"What night?"_

_"The night my mother died."_

Priya went quiet.

Maya looked at the photograph. _"I was six. I was in this room."_ She closed her eyes. For a moment, there was nothing. Then a memory broke through the dark.

Her mother was lying in bed. Little Maya was standing right beside her. But someone else was standing in the corner of the room. Maya opened her eyes. _"There was someone else here."_

Priya stared at her. _"Who?"_

Maya shook her head. _"I don't know."_

She looked down at the photograph in her hand. Something was written on the back. Maya slowly turned it over.

#### **DON'T WAKE HER.**
...

Maya stared at the words.

_"Who wrote this?"_ Priya asked.

Maya shook her head. _"I don't know."_ She looked at the photograph again. _"But I think I've seen this handwriting before."_

Priya looked at her. _"Where?"_

Maya didn't answer. She walked to an old box near her wardrobe, pulled it out, and opened it. Inside were photographs from her childhood. Maya went through them with trembling fingers. Then she stopped.

She pulled out an old photograph of her mother. She turned it over. Her hands began to shake violently. There was a date written on the back.

**The exact night her mother died.**

Maya stared at it, the pieces snapping together with terrifying clarity. Then she remembered the room. Her mother lying in bed. Six-year-old Maya standing beside her. And the presence in the corner.

Maya closed her eyes. She tried to remember what happened next.
Just the dark corner. And then a voice.

Very soft.  
Very close.

Maya opened her eyes. _"I heard something that night."_

Priya looked at her, breathless. _"What?"_

Maya shook her head. _"I don't remember."_

...

**Then the camera clicked.**

Both of them spun around. A fresh photograph slid out of the Polaroid. Maya didn't move. Priya reached down and picked it up. She waited for it to develop.

When the image cleared, Priya’s face went entirely pale.

_"Maya…"_

Maya took the photograph. At first, she didn't understand what she was looking at. It showed her childhood bedroom. Her mother lying in bed. Little Maya standing beside her.

Then Maya noticed something else in the background. The figure from the corner was no longer in the shadows. It was leaning over adult Maya’s bed—in the present day.

Maya looked at the real bed behind her. It was empty. She looked back at the photograph.

The figure in the white nightdress was standing right where adult Maya had been sleeping just moments ago.

Maya dropped the photograph. Her breath caught in her throat. _"I remember,"_ she whispered.

Priya looked at her, terrified. _"Remember what?"_

Maya stared at the photograph on the floor, finally understanding the note written on the back. _"The warning wasn't for my mother. It was a rule for me. I stayed asleep, ignorant of what happened for twenty years... and that's why it left me alone."_

Priya looked at her, eyes wide. _"And the photos?"_

Maya looked down at the camera, realizing too late what her curiosity had done. _"By taking these pictures... I woke myself up."_

A heavy, suffocating silence fell over the room. The air turned freezing cold, frosting the edges of the windowpane.

Then, Maya heard it.

Right beside her ear. Warm breath against her skin. A voice, soft and ancient, whispering the ancient rule.

**_"NOW IT'S YOUR TURN TO SLEEP."_**
