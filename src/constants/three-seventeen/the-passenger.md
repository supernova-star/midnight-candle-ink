**_“Keep going straight, Sameer.”_**

The heavy words hung thick and frozen in the damp air inside the cab. Sameer kept his eyes glued to the dark, wet road ahead, his foot pressing down heavy on the gas pedal. The squeaking windshield wipers swept back and forth against the glass with a loud, rhythmic thump-thump, thump-thump, desperately trying to clear away the blinding sheets of heavy rain.

He glanced down at the dashboard. The bright digital numbers still glowed in an eerie green light: **3:19 AM**
 
A cold, nervous sweat formed at the back of his neck, making his shirt collar stick uncomfortably to his skin. By his own count, they had been driving for what felt like ten long, exhausting minutes through the completely empty and deserted streets.

Sameer looked back down at the small clock again.

**3:19 AM.**

The time hadn't moved forward—not even by a single second.

...

_“You’re not from around here, are you, sahab?”_ Sameer asked, trying his best to break the heavy, suffocating silence that had settled inside the cab. His voice shook just a little bit, giving away the calm face he was desperately trying to put on.

For a long moment, the passenger said absolutely nothing. In the rearview mirror, his eyes caught the pale, eerie green glow of the taxi meter. They looked completely flat and cold, like two smooth, dark stones that reflected none of the rainy night outside.

_“Does it matter where a journey starts,”_ the man replied softly, _“when the road never changes?”_

Sameer frowned, a cold, hard knot forming deep in his stomach. He tightened his grip on the steering wheel until his palms felt slick and wet with sweat, staring out hard at the empty, dark road ahead.
 
_“Look, sahab, I have a job to do, but this road is leading us nowhere,”_ Sameer said, his heart beating faster. _“We’ve passed that exact same broken, flickering streetlamp three times already.”_
 
The passenger tilted his head slightly in the deep shadow of the back seat. _“Have we?”_ 
 
There was something strange and deeply unsettling about the way he said it, almost as if the idea genuinely surprised him. 
 
Sameer glanced back at him through the mirror. The dark shadows covering the man's face seemed to twist and stretch, even though the dim, fading light inside the cab hadn't changed at all.
 
_“Or are you only just noticing how small your world has become tonight?”_ the passenger asked in a low whisper.

Sameer didn't reply. He snapped his eyes back to the road ahead, a freezing-cold feeling creeping all the way up his spine. Suddenly, all he wanted in the world was for the man to just stop talking.

...

Sameer couldn't take it for another second. The suffocating silence inside the cab felt far too heavy, squeezing his chest like a tight metal band. He slammed his foot down hard on the brakes.

The white cab skidded violently across the rain-soaked road. The tires made a loud, scraping scream against the wet pavement before coming to a sudden, jarring stop right beneath the buzzing, broken streetlamp.

Sameer stared straight into the rearview mirror, his breathing ragged and heavy. In the dark glass, the passenger’s face remained completely hidden in the deep shadow—only the vague outline of his shoulders and his pale, thin hands were visible. All of Sameer's exhaustion had vanished in an instant. Pure panic had taken its place, making his heart hammer wildly against his ribs.

_“Enough of these games!”_ he snapped, his voice cracking under the pressure. _“Who are you? What do you want from me?”_

The passenger didn't even flinch. He slowly raised one pale hand and tapped a single time against the glass partition between them.

**Tap.**

_“You wanted double the money, Sameer,”_ the man whispered. His voice was incredibly low, cold, and hollow, echoing from the dark. _“You wanted a quick way out of the rent. But some debts aren't paid with paper or coins.”_

Sameer stared at the reflection in the glass, his breath catching painfully in his throat.

_“What... what are you talking about?”_ Sameer stammered.

_“Look at the clock, Sameer.”_

Slowly, with his shoulders trembling from fear, Sameer turned his head toward the dashboard. The glowing green numbers blurred together for a hot moment before settling rigidly back into place.

**3:19 AM.**

Then, the car radio, which Sameer had switched completely off hours ago, suddenly crackled to life with a sharp, deafening burst of loud static.
