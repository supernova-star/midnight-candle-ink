**A dull amber glow cut through the pitch darkness where the clock had reset.**

**3:17 AM.**

The engine suddenly roared back to life with a violent shake, making the entire white cab tremble beneath him.vThe headlights flashed on, cutting into a thick curtain of heavy rain that rattled loudly against the roof and glass.

Sameer gasped frantically for air. His chest heaved as his lungs burned, feeling as though he had just surfaced from deep, freezing water. Cold sweat ran down his face, stinging his eyes. His hands were locked tightly around the steering wheel, his knuckles turned white and stiff from the pressure.

The car was already moving.

It rolled forward along a slick, empty road that Sameer didn't recognize. Something about it felt horribly wrong—as if an unseen hand was controlling the car, and the taxi already knew where it was heading. Sameer looked down at the glowing dashboard.

The clock was ticking forward again.

**3:19 AM.**

Panic rushed through his veins. Sameer stomped hard on the brake pedal.

Nothing happened.

The pedal went completely soft and loose under his foot, offering no resistance at all. He pumped it again, harder this time, but the taxi didn't slow down a bit. His leg felt heavy and strangely disconnected from his body, like it wasn't his own.

The needle on the speedometer began to climb—past sixty, then seventy. Sameer yanked desperately at the steering wheel, trying to pull off the road, but the tires locked in place. The cab kept speeding straight ahead into the black night.

...

Suddenly, the windshield wipers jammed mid-swipe and stopped dead.

The steady thump-thump against the wet glass vanished, replaced instantly by the ear-splitting screech of rubber tearing across asphalt outside. The headlights caught a sudden, blinding flash of metal in the dark.

A ghostly dark 1990s Maruti sedan materialized out of thin air, barreling straight toward them and crashing violently into the side of Sameer’s cab.

It was the exact same dark car Sameer had owned thirty years ago—the very car he was driving the night he struck the man and fled into the dark.

Sameer gasped as a surge of terror tore through his chest. The passenger-side window exploded inward, showering the cab in a hail of sharp glass.

A heavy, wet leather briefcase smashed through the opening, slamming hard onto the dashboard. It slid across the plastic, leaving a thick, dark smear of blood right in front of Sameer's eyes.

Sameer stared at the stain, completely frozen in horror.

_“I’m sorry... I’m sorry...”_ Sameer whimpered, his voice trembling uncontrollably.

_“Sorry doesn't turn back the clock,”_ the voice behind him replied, flat, cold, and emotionless.

The digital display on the dashboard began to flicker wildly again. The glowing numbers scrambled into meaningless green dashes for a second, then snapped still:

**3:19 AM**

...

A familiar smell drifted from the darkness behind him—damp earth, old wool, and the heavy, metallic stench of blood.

Sameer stopped breathing.

The air inside the cab turned bone-chilling cold in a single second. White frost began to spread around the edges of the side windows, creeping across the glass like tiny spiders.

He tried to scream, but his jaw was locked tight. Fear held him completely still. Slowly, he forced himself to look up. His terrified eyes met the rearview mirror.

Two lifeless eyes stared back at him from the dark of the back seat. The dark wool coat was there again, heavy and dripping wet. A thin, cold smile crept across the passenger’s face.

Then the voice came—soft, low, and terrifyingly close, as if it were speaking right inside Sameer’s mind.

_“Keep going straight, Sameer.”_

Sameer’s chest tightened, squeezed as if wrapped in heavy iron bands.

**_“We have a long way to go.”_**
