**Sharma’s threats echoed louder than the midnight rain.**

_“I’m telling you, Sameer,”_ Sharma warned, his voice cutting through the static. _“Double the rent from next month, or get your cab out of my lot!”_

Sameer protested, _“You can’t just double it overnight, Sharma! Where am I supposed to get that kind of money?”_ 

_“That’s your problem, driver!”_ snapped Sharma.

The line went dead with a sharp click.

The argument had happened hours ago, but Sameer could still feel the anger sitting tight in his chest. His throat was dry, his head was pounding with a dull ache, and all he wanted was to finish his shift and go home. Instead, he was still behind the wheel, trapped in the night.

The rain hadn't stopped all night. Heavy drops slapped hard against the windshield, forcing the wipers to sweep back and forth with a steady thump-thump sound. Sameer rubbed his tired eyes, feeling the sting of exhaustion, and checked the small clock on the dashboard.

**3:17 AM.**

He had been driving for hours. He'd taken three different routes across the city, passing shuttered shops, dark empty intersections, and the occasional delivery scooter rushing through puddles. But tonight, something felt different. He couldn't explain it. The city simply felt... quiet.

Dead quiet.

Sameer turned his white cab into a narrow side street. Shuttered shops lined both sides, their wet dark windows reflecting the rain. A few old brick buildings stood behind them, barely visible through the thick mist. There wasn't a single person on the street. A broken streetlamp buzzed overhead like a trapped fly, flickering every few seconds and throwing pale patches of light across the wet road. Sameer slowed down.

Then he saw someone standing near the curb.

...

A man stood beside the crumbling archway of an old mill, half-hidden in the deep darkness. He slowly raised one pale hand.

Sameer pressed down hard on the brakes. The white taxi came to a sudden stop right against the curb, its tires making a sharp, wet hiss against the slick road. The heavy back door clicked open, and a blast of freezing, damp air rushed inside. The silent stranger slipped into the back seat without saying a single word.

There was something deeply strange about the way he moved. He was completely quiet, moving like a shadow with almost no weight at all. Even though the heavy rain was pouring down outside, his long dark coat was bone dry. All at once, a thick, choking smell of wet dirt and old, musty wool filled the cramped car.

Sameer turned slightly and looked at him through the rearview mirror. _“Where to, sahab?”_

The man didn't answer. Instead, he stared at the small digital clock on the dashboard. 3:17 AM. Sameer waited, the quiet air growing heavy.

Finally, the man spoke. _“Just keep going straight, Sameer.”_

Sameer froze. A cold chill ran down his neck. His hands tightened around the steering wheel. For a long second, he couldn't even catch his breath. He knew for a fact he had never told this man his name.

...

_“Sorry... did I tell you my name?”_ Sameer asked. His voice was much quieter now, barely louder than a soft whisper. He stared hard at the strange passenger through the glass of the rearview mirror.

_“You didn’t have to,”_ the man said. His eyes caught the sharp green glow of the taxi meter.

Sameer tried to force a quick laugh, but it came out sounding tight, strained, and completely hollow. _“Working these late-night hours, you see a lot of strange people. But knowing a driver's name without being told? That’s definitely new.”_

_“Some names are hard to forget,”_ the man replied in a flat tone.
 
Sameer frowned, feeling a small knot form in his stomach. _“What does that mean?”_

The man looked out through the rain-streaked window into the black night. _“History has a way of staying on the road.”_ A thin smile appeared on his face. 

Sameer couldn't see his eyes clearly anymore. The back seat suddenly seemed much darker than it should have been, completely swallowing up the dim yellow light from the streetlamps.

_“What’s that supposed to mean?”_ Sameer asked again, his chest tightening up with real fear.

The man didn't answer. Instead, he leaned back into the shadows. _“We have a long way to go.”_

Sameer tightened his grip on the steering wheel. His knuckles turned pale against the dark leather.

_“Just drive,”_ the voice from the dark commanded.

Sameer swallowed hard, his throat dry. He shifted the heavy gear stick and pulled the white cab away from the dark curb. The empty, lonely street stretched out far ahead of them, wet and shining like a mirror under the pouring rain. For a few long, quiet seconds, neither of them said a word.

Then Sameer glanced at the dashboard. **3:19 AM**.
