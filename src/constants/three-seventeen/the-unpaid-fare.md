**The engine hummed steadily as the white taxi climbed the steep flyover.**

Sameer looked ahead through the wet windshield and slowly realized where they were.

The Lalbaug bridge.

He knew this road by heart, but tonight, something about it felt deeply wrong.

The skyline around them didn't look right. The tall modern towers along the horizon were gone, swallowed up by the night. In their place stood rows of low rooftops, dim old shops, and crumbling structures that Sameer hadn't seen in decades.

Cold sweat dripped down his forehead. He tightened his trembling hands around the steering wheel until his muscles ached. For a few long seconds, he couldn't even speak.

Then, through the rain, he noticed a shadow standing right in the middle of the empty road ahead.

The figure raised one pale hand, signaling for the cab to stop.

Sameer slowed down, his heart racing against his ribs. As they rolled closer, a flickering yellow streetlamp lit the man's face. Sameer's breath caught sharply in his chest.

He knew that face.

The man was holding a heavy, old leather briefcase. There was a dark, wet stain smeared across it.

Sameer stared at him through the glass, frozen with fear.

Suddenly, a terrifying memory rushed back to him.

Thirty years ago.  
The dark road.  
The loud, sickening impact.  
The body lying motionless on the wet pavement.

And Sameer pressing the gas, driving away into the darkness.

He had never told a soul. He had buried the guilt so deep that, over the years, he had almost convinced himself it had never happened. Now that exact same man was standing right in the middle of the road.

Panicked, Sameer slammed his foot down on the brake pedal.

...

The brake pedal went completely soft beneath his foot, giving no resistance at all.

The taxi didn't slow down. It glided straight through the man without a bump, slicing right through him like he was made of thin, grey smoke.

The tires just kept rolling over the wet road with a low, steady splash, as if nothing had been standing there at all.

Sameer gasped, cold air burning deep in his lungs. His eyes snapped up to the rearview mirror. The rain-soaked road behind them was completely empty. The figure was gone from the bridge. Then Sameer's gaze shifted down to the back seat in the glass. The man was sitting right behind him.

Sameer froze, a hard lump of terror locking his throat. Water dripped from the passenger’s dark coat onto the seat, but his face remained in shadow. Only his eyes were clear—hollow, dark, and locked onto Sameer’s reflection with an icy, unforgiving stare.

_“You never paid the true fare, Sameer,”_ a cold voice whispered, sounding as if it came from right beside his ear.

Sameer let out a raw, ragged scream and dug his fingernails into the rubber steering wheel.

Outside the windshield, the world began to warp.

The wet road stretched out long and thin. The yellow streetlamps melted into long, bleeding lines of light across the glass.

One by one, the concrete walls of the bridge faded away, swallowed by a deep, pitch-black void until there was nothing left outside the windows. The sound of rain tapping on the metal roof completely stopped.

There was no city. No sky. No road beneath them.

Only the heavy, unnatural hum of the engine echoed in the dark.

...

Sameer tried to cry out, but no sound came. His voice was trapped tight in his throat, his chest feeling as if it had turned to solid ice.

From behind the driver's partition, a heavy, bone-chilling hand reached around.

It clamped down onto his left shoulder, squeezing with a brutal weight that made his bones ache as if they were about to snap.

Sameer stared straight ahead through the windshield, but there was nothing left to see.

The bridge was completely gone. The patter of rain against the glass had vanished. The soft rumble of rubber against asphalt had fallen dead silent. There was only an endless, pitch-black void wrapping around the cab, swallowing it whole.

With shaking eyes, Sameer forced his gaze down to the dashboard. The glowing green numbers on the digital clock began to twitch and scramble. They spun wildly, flashing through random lines before dropping straight past the frozen time. The display gave one last violent flicker, then settled still:

**0:00**

The clock had reset back to nothing.
