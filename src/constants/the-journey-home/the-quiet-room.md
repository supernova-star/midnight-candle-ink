**The alarm rang at 4 AM.**

Buku opened his eyes in the dark. The ceiling fan hummed. The flat in Gurgaon was silent, with no dhaak, no radio, no voices. His pillow was wet, and he did not know when the tears had started.

He was forty years old. He ran a team of thirty people. He wore a smart watch and drove a good car, and nobody in this city had ever called him anything but Buku, or Sir.

His mother had stopped calling him Monai years ago. When he moved to Gurgaon, he had asked her gently, _"Ma, please, in front of my colleagues..."_ She had laughed and said, _"All right, Buku."_ After that, it was always Buku.

He had felt proud then. He had felt grown.

Now he sat on the edge of the bed in the quiet room, just as Thakuma had said, and he wanted so badly to hear that one small name.

Beside him, his wife Riya stirred. _"Buku? Why are you awake?"_

_"It's Mahalaya,"_ he said. His voice came out rough.

She sat up and looked at his face. She did not ask anything. She only reached out and wiped his cheek with her thumb.

_"You set a reminder weeks ago,"_ she said softly. _"I saw it. I didn't want to say."_

He found the old recording on his phone and played it low. The deep voice filled the room as it once had filled the whole Bari. Riya leaned her head on his shoulder. She had only ever seen the Bari in wedding photos, but she listened as if she knew every word.

Soft footsteps came from the door. Aditi, eight years old, stood there rubbing her eyes.

_"Baba, what's this music?"_

He held out her arm, and she curled up between them. When the recording ended, she whispered, _"It sounds like a very old story."_

_"It is,"_ he said. _"Do you want to hear the rest?"_

She nodded, and he began to tell her about the Bari, and the dalan, and Thakuma, and a small name that meant my heart's own.

Riya smiled in the dark. _"So that's why your mother never stops saying it on the phone."_

_"You heard that?"_

_"Every time. I always thought it was sweet."_

That evening he called his mother in Kolkata.

_"Ma, what did Thakuma put in the khichuri?"_

She laughed softly. _"Just love, and a lot of ghee. Why?"_

He closed his eyes. For a moment he could almost hear the old house around her.

_"No reason, Ma,"_ he said. "Just missing it."

_"Monai,"_ she said gently, and this time he did not mind at all.

That Saturday, he cooked khichuri with Aditi while Riya laid out the steel plates. He fed his daughter with his own hand, telling her about the clever jackal. She made a face at the first bite, then asked for more.

Then he opened his laptop and booked three tickets for Pujo, one for each of them, to go home.
