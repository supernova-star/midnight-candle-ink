**He was lifted high into the air.**

It was Dadu. Buku sat on his grandfather's shoulders in the middle of the thakur dalan, which was now full of people. Women in red-bordered saris. Men in white dhutis. Children running everywhere. Incense hung thick in the air.

_"Look carefully,"_ Dadu said, pointing. _"She is looking at you."_

The white cloth was gone. Maa Durga stood tall and glowing, with ten arms and a fierce, loving face. Her eyes had been drawn. They were huge and deep and kind, and for one moment Buku felt she was looking only at him.

_"Dadu,"_ he whispered. _"Will she remember me?"_

_"Maa remembers everyone who comes home."_

The day passed in a blur. Luchi and aloo dum for breakfast. Sandesh from a big box that nobody could open until Dadu said so. Cousins fighting over who would carry the prasad. And all day long, voices calling:

_"Monai!"_

_"Monai, come here!"_

_"Monai, eat more!"_

He still rolled his eyes. But a little less than before.

That evening, the lights went out for the aarti. The whole family climbed to the roof and sat under a sky full of stars, eating hot khichuri from steel plates. The dhaak was still beating far away. Thakuma sat beside him with her hand on his head. She smelled of ghee and old sandalwood.

_"Monai,"_ Ma called from across the roof, _"eat more! You're too thin!"_

_"Yes, Ma,"_ he said quietly.

He didn't mind. And he thought, the way only a child can, that this would go on forever.

Then the dhaak began to grow faint.

Thakuma's hand grew light on his head. The stars blurred. Dadu's voice came from very far away, saying something he could not hear.

_"Thakuma?"_ Buku called. _"Thakuma, wait..."_

Somewhere, very near and very strange, a small sharp sound began to ring.
