**_"Monai! Come and eat!"_**

Ten-year-old Buku froze in the middle of the lane. His friends from the next house, who were playing marbles, all turned and grinned.

_"Monai!"_ one of them sang. _"Your Ma is calling!"_

_"Don't call me that!"_ Buku shouted, his ears burning.

But the voice came again, louder, from the balcony of the old Bari. _"Monai! Rice is getting cold!"_

The Bari was the family's big old house in North Kolkata. It was a hundred years old, with thick walls, wooden doors, and a huge open hall at its heart called the thakur dalan. The hall was built only for Durga Puja. For eleven months it stood quiet, and every year, right about now, it began to wake up.

Buku stomped inside. The house was already busy. Uncles carried bamboo poles. Aunts sorted new clothes. Cousins ran up and down the stairs.

_"Monai, hold this."_

_"Monai, where are your slippers?"_

_"Monai, you've become so thin!"_

Every voice in the Bari said that name. He had heard it all day, every day, since he could walk.

_"I'm Buku,"_ he told his cousin Tuli in the corridor. _"Buku! Nobody is saying it right!"_

_"But you are Monai,"_ Tuli said, laughing. _"It's a baby name."_

That was the last straw. Buku ran to the roof and sat in the corner, hugging his knees. The sun was going down. The sky was orange. Far away, someone was testing a dhaak.

Soft footsteps came up the stairs. He knew them without looking.

_"Thakuma, please go away."_

_"I'm only here for the breeze."_ She sat beside him, her bangles clinking. _"My knees hurt from all those stairs, you know."_

He said nothing. She said nothing. A pigeon landed on the wall and left again.

_"Tomorrow is Mahalaya,"_ she said at last. _"Wake up at four. I will come for you."_

_"Why so early?"_

_"Because Maa Durga starts her journey home in the dark, and we must welcome her."_ She smiled and pinched his cheek. _"And tomorrow, Monai, I will tell you why you have that name. I have never told anyone. Only you."_

He looked at her, surprised. His anger had gone quiet.

_"Why tomorrow?"_ he asked.

She stood slowly and held out her hand. _"Because some stories can only be told in front of Maa."_

That night, Buku lay under the mosquito net, listening to the Bari breathe. Somewhere below, his mother laughed. Somewhere above, the old clock struck ten.

He fell asleep wondering what secret his Thakuma had been keeping for ten years.
