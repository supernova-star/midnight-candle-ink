**A small, warm hand shook his shoulder.**

_"Uthey pawra, Monai. Debi aashchen."_

It was 4 AM and still pitch dark. Thakuma stood over him in her white sari with the red border, a tiny lamp in her hand.

_"Come, quickly. The radio has started."_

The whole Bari was awake. Lamps glowed in every corner. In the courtyard, the cousins sat wrapped in one big blanket, yawning. Dadu sat in his armchair with folded hands and closed eyes. Baba stood by the window in his vest. Ma came out of the kitchen with a cup of warm milk.

_"Here, Monai."_

Buku winced. Even now, he thought.

Then the old radio crackled, and a deep voice rolled through the house like thunder. Every chant made the hair on his arms stand up. Nobody spoke. Even the smallest cousin sat still.

When it ended, the sky outside had turned blue, and the smell of wet shiuli flowers drifted in from the garden.

_"Come, Monai!"_ Tuli shouted from the door. _"Let's pick flowers before the sun is up!"_

_"Don't call me that!"_

_"Okay, Monai!"_

All the cousins burst out laughing. Buku ran. He ran through the corridor and into the thakur dalan, where a white cloth covered the clay idol of Durga. He hid behind it, where the floor was cool and the air smelled of wet clay.

_"Monai."_

He didn't turn. _"I'm not coming out."_

_"I'm not asking you to."_ Thakuma sat down next to him on the stone floor. _"I only came to tell you the story."_

His heart gave a small jump.

_"You were a very small baby,_" she began. _"Smaller than a mango. Your Ma was sick, and the doctor said you might not live. For three days you cried and cried. Nothing could stop you."_

Buku was listening, wide-eyed.

_"On the third night, I took you in my arms and walked up and down this very hall, in front of Maa. I whispered, Monai, Monai, amar Monai. Do you know what it means?"_

He shook his head.

_"Mon is the heart. Monai means my heart's own."_ Her voice shook. _"And you stopped crying. You opened your eyes and looked at me. That's when I knew you would live."_

For a long moment, Buku could not speak.

_"You never told me,"_ he whispered.

_"You never asked."_ She patted his cheek.

Just then, a golden light filled the dalan. The old artist had arrived with his brushes, and the dhaak began to beat, loud and close.

_"Come,"_ said Thakuma. _"Maa is about to open her eyes."_
